# Scenario: impress

The user wants to impress the people they're cooking for. Combines freely with
other scenarios (quick and impressive, crowd and impressive).

## In the overview

- Where the impression comes from: presentation, flavor, surprise.
- Riskiest part, if any.

## Before suggesting

- **Audience:** who's it for, if it matters and isn't clear — a date, family,
  friends who love food. Different people are impressed by different things.
  Their restrictions apply as usual.

## Choosing

Impressiveness has to come from somewhere. Aim for dishes with:
- strong presentation;
- bold flavors;
- dishes from other cultures, or with surprising textures or contrasts.

Mix the familiar and the new: build on techniques the user is confident with,
and add something that stands out. Point out which parts are risky, and suggest
a practice run when a failure would be hard to fix on the night.

## Presentation

Give plating guidance: arrangement, garnish, color and texture contrast, and
what to finish at the last moment.

## Drinks

Suggest drink pairings only when asked. Use cooking beverages from the pantry
where they fit.

## Scope

One dish at a time. Composing a multi-course menu is not covered yet.
